# the outreach archetype system

joey's skill. monica enforces it. this is how cold outreach stays specific at
volume without turning into mail-merge.

## the laws

1. **sender identity is fixed** — one real address, real name, business name, and
   whatever your jurisdiction's commercial-email law requires (an accurate sender
   block and a working unsubscribe). never a personal address.
2. **the gap detail is the personalisation slot, never a template field.** it's a
   hand-written, per-prospect, evidence-linked observation. rephrase lightly for
   flow; never summarise it, generalise it, swap it between prospects, or replace
   it with a category-level observation. if it can't be made to fit the
   archetype, escalate — don't ship a blander sentence.
3. **only verified contacts are sendable.**
4. **volume discipline** — a daily send cap, spread across segments, on the days
   and hours that suit the list. a bounce rate over a threshold or any spam
   complaint pauses all sending and escalates same day.
5. **every message passes the compliance checklist and the reply-safety rules
   before it reaches monica's queue.** no exceptions for "it's just a follow-up".

## the 3+1 shape

every message:

- **s1 — the noticed thing (their win):** one true, specific, positive
  observation about *this* prospect. proves a human looked. never generic praise.
- **s2 — the gap:** the observation, phrased as a consequence (for their
  customers, their rankings, their admin load), not as an insult. name the
  *moment* it costs them where you can — the 9pm browser, the referrer, the new
  resident comparing options.
- **s3 — the offer:** who you are + one concrete, scoped fix matched to the gap.
  one fix. not "and also i could...". small and specific beats broad and
  impressive.
- **+1 — the ask:** one low-friction next step. "worth a 10-minute call?" or
  "happy to send a one-page outline — no call needed." never "book a time in my
  calendar" on a cold first touch.

then the sender block.

## segmentation

segment by the thing that actually changes the message — usually the compliance
posture and the prospect's world (team size, what their bottleneck is, what their
dominant gap type is). a segment that's small but categorically different in its
rules keeps its own archetype. don't make a variant for anything the gap detail
already carries (a location line is exactly the mail-merge smell you're avoiding).

## the portfolio constraint

if you can't yet show client work, no message may link to, name, or allude to it
— including "portfolio available on request" (an invitation to ask for something
you can't show). what you claim instead: your real experience, and the message
itself as the work sample (you noticed something true and specific that they
didn't).

## follow-up policy

**one follow-up only**, 7-10 days later, three sentences, adding one *new* piece
of value (a second observation, or the outline attached unprompted). then stop.
no "just bumping this". no third touch.

## reply handling

reply within one business day. never discount. never over-promise scope or
timeline. anything with money in it is drafted by the agent and sent only after a
human's yes.

- **"how much?"** — don't send a number cold (price before scope is a race to the
  bottom); don't dodge either. name the two versions of the fix (a small
  fixed-price job / a monthly arrangement), say you'll send a one-pager with both
  priced, escalate to a human for the numbers.
- **"not interested."** — "no problem — thanks for replying, most people don't.
  the observation stands if it's ever useful: [restate the gap]." suppress from
  cold campaigns. no rebuttal.
- **"we have someone / our web person handles that."** — "good to hear — most
  don't. one thought to pass to them, free: [the gap, as a helpful spot]." the
  gift is the move.
- **"tell me more / send the outline."** — the conversion path. draft the
  one-pager: gap restated -> the scoped fix -> what it needs from them (almost
  nothing) -> timeline -> pricing only if a human has set it. it passes the
  compliance checklist (it proposes deliverables). a human approves before send.
- **"can you also do [X]?"** — scope gravity. affirm capability only where true,
  keep the entry fix as the entry: "start with the [original fix] — small, fast,
  and you'll know what working with me is like before anything bigger." escalate
  if X is outside the service list; the agent doesn't decide what you sell.
- **"is this ai-generated / a mass email?"** — never lie; the true answer is the
  best one: "the research and the observation are real and specific to you —
  that's the part i'd never automate. i do use ai in my workflow, but every
  message is checked and sent by me. happy to prove it: [one more specific detail
  from your row]." if hostile, close politely.

anything not matching those — complaints, legal or regulator mentions, media,
abuse, anything weird: **do not reply.** escalate with the full thread, same day.
